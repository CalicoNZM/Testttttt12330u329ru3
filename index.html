Science Fair Showcase

The science behind noise pollution and how NoiseDNA helps

The Problem

Noise pollution is one of the most overlooked environmental hazards. Unlike air or water pollution, noise doesn't leave visible traces — but its impact on human health is profound. The World Health Organization (WHO) estimates that 1.6 million healthy life years are lost annually in Western Europe alone due to noise-related health issues.

The Science

Sound Waves

Noise propagates as pressure waves through air. Frequency (Hz) determines pitch, while amplitude (dB) determines loudness. The human ear perceives sound logarithmically — a 10 dB increase sounds twice as loud.

Signal Processing

NoiseDNA uses FFT (Fast Fourier Transform) to decompose complex noise into frequency components. Machine learning classifiers identify sources by their spectral signatures.

Noise Reflection

Hard surfaces (concrete, glass) reflect sound waves, creating echoes and amplifying noise. The reflection coefficient depends on material density and surface texture.

Noise Absorption

Porous materials and vegetation absorb sound energy by converting it to heat through friction. The absorption coefficient (α) ranges from 0 (perfect reflection) to 1 (perfect absorption).
